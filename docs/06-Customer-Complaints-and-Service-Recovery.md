# Customer Complaints and Service Recovery

**The Lakeside Hotel**  
Lake Macquarie, NSW  
Document 06 | Version 1.0 | November 2026  
Internal use only

## 1. Purpose

A complaint is a chance to put something right and keep a guest's trust. Handle it calmly, fairly, and in line with this procedure. Do not argue, and do not ignore it and hope the guest leaves.

## 2. The LAST steps

Use **LAST** every time: Listen, Apologise, Solve, Thank.

### 2.1 Listen

1. Stop what you can safely leave. Face the guest. Give them your full attention.
2. Let them finish. Do not talk over them or explain the kitchen's side while they are still talking.
3. Stay calm, even if they are not. Lower your voice rather than raising it.
4. If the area is public and they are upset, offer to move to a quieter spot near the host stand or office, but do not take a visibly angry person into a confined room alone. Tell a colleague where you are.
5. Confirm what you heard: "Just so I've got this right, the steak is overcooked and it took longer than you expected."
6. Note the table number, time, names if offered, and what was ordered.

### 2.2 Apologise

1. Apologise for the experience, sincerely and without drama.
2. Use wording like: "I'm sorry this wasn't up to the standard we want for you."
3. Do not blame the guest, a co-worker, the kitchen, "the system," or "head office."
4. Do not admit legal liability, promise insurance outcomes, or say "it's all our fault and we'll pay all your costs." If the complaint involves injury, illness, discrimination, or a threat of legal action, get the Duty Manager (ext. 201) immediately and leave those words to them.
5. Do not make a joke of the complaint.

### 2.3 Solve

1. Ask what would put it right: "Let me fix this. What can I do that would help?"
2. Offer a practical fix first, then a comp if it is needed and you have the authority (section 3).
3. Good first fixes include:
   - remaking a drink or meal at once
   - replacing an incorrect or poor item
   - correcting a wrong bill
   - moving a table if one is free
   - fixing a simple comfort issue (heater, fan, spill, music volume in that zone)
4. Tell the guest what you are going to do, then do it. If you need to check with the kitchen or the Duty Manager, say how long you will be and come back in that time.
5. If you cannot solve it within your authority, say so honestly and get the Duty Manager. Do not invent a promise the venue cannot keep.
6. Check back after the fix: "Has that made it right?"

### 2.4 Thank

1. Thank the guest for telling you. You cannot fix a problem you do not know about.
2. A simple close is enough: "Thanks for raising it. Please tell me if anything else isn't right."
3. If the Duty Manager has approved a follow-up (a call or a note the next day), record that in the log and do not promise a follow-up you will not make.

## 3. When to comp

A "comp" is a reduction, removal, or complimentary item on a bill. It is not automatic, and it is not a punishment for the kitchen.

**Staff (including new starters)**

- You may remake a drink, replace a meal, or replace a clearly wrong item.
- You may offer a complimentary tea, coffee, or soft drink while the guest waits for a remake.
- You must not take an item off the bill, apply a discount, or comp alcohol unless the Duty Manager has authorised it.
- Tell the Duty Manager about every remake or replacement, even if the guest then seems happy.

**Duty Manager (ext. 201)**

- May comp or discount up to $50 on a single account, or a meal or round of drinks within that amount, where that fairly fixes the problem.
- May authorise a courtesy drink or dessert above a staff-level remake.
- Must record the reason, the amount, the table or guest name if known, and the POS docket or comp ticket.
- Must not comp liquor to a person who appears intoxicated, or as a way to settle an RSA issue. See Document 02.

**Licensee or Approved Manager**

- Approves anything above $50, any goodwill offer for a future visit, any group or function refund, and any complaint that may involve injury, illness, or a legal claim.

**Do not comp when:**

- the guest is intoxicated and the "fix" would be more liquor
- the demand is aggressive or unsafe — get the Duty Manager and, if needed, follow Document 02
- you are being asked to wipe a whole table's bill for a small issue and you have not checked with the Duty Manager
- you have no docket or record, and you would have to alter a till without a manager

If a guest says they will post a bad review unless you comp the bill, stay polite, do not bargain under threat, and get the Duty Manager.

## 4. If the guest is angry or the issue is serious

Get the **Duty Manager (ext. 201)** immediately if:

- the guest is shouting, threatening, or abusive to staff or other patrons
- the complaint is about a fall, injury, alleged assault, or feeling unsafe
- the complaint is about food-borne illness or a serious allergy incident
- the complaint is about discrimination, refusal of service, or a minor
- more than one table is involved, or a function is unhappy
- you feel out of your depth, or the guest asks for "the manager" and will not deal with you

Until the Duty Manager arrives:

1. Stay polite and keep listening.
2. Do not put your hands on the guest or block their way, unless you must move out of immediate danger.
3. If other patrons are at risk, or the person is intoxicated, violent, quarrelsome, or disorderly, follow Document 02 and do not try to "smooth it over" with a free drink.

## 5. Logging complaints

Log every complaint that is more than a quick remake the guest was immediately happy with. When in doubt, log it.

The Duty Manager keeps the complaints log in the office, alongside the incident register. Use the incident register instead (or as well) if the matter involves RSA, a refusal, injury, police, or a safety incident (see Document 02).

**Record as soon as you can, and before the end of the shift:**

1. Date, time, and location (table number, bar, beer garden, gaming room).
2. Guest name and contact, if they are willing to give them. Do not insist.
3. Staff involved and any witnesses.
4. What the guest said the problem was, in their words as closely as you can.
5. What you saw or checked (for example, the steak was well done; the ticket was delayed 25 minutes).
6. What was offered and what was accepted, including any comp amount and the POS reference.
7. Whether the guest left happy, unhappy, or wanted a follow-up.
8. Anything the opening team or Licensee should know.

Be factual. Do not write insults about the guest or a co-worker. Do not post about the complaint on social media or discuss it with other patrons.

The Duty Manager reviews the log during the end-of-night report (Document 01) and flags any pattern (the same dish, the same delay, the same fault) for the next briefing.

## 6. Quick wording you can use

**Opening**

> "I'm sorry you had to wait. Tell me what happened and I'll sort it out."

**Checking you understood**

> "So the meal came out later than expected, and the steak isn't cooked the way you asked. Have I got that right?"

**Going to get help**

> "I want to get this right, and I'll need the Duty Manager for that part. I'll be back in a moment."

**Closing**

> "Thanks for telling us. The replacement is on the way, and I've taken that item off while you wait. Please flag me if it still isn't right."

## 7. After the guest has gone

1. Tell the kitchen or the bar, calmly and privately, what was wrong so it is not repeated. Do this as a fact, not a blame session.
2. Make sure the comp or remake is on the POS correctly.
3. Complete the log.
4. If you are rattled, take a short moment off the floor with the Duty Manager's say-so before you go back to other tables.

# Opening and Closing Checklist

**The Lakeside Hotel**  
Lake Macquarie, NSW  
Document 01 | Version 1.0 | November 2026  
Internal use only

## 1. Purpose

Use this checklist every trading day to open and close the venue safely and consistently.

## 2. Who is responsible

- **Duty Manager (ext. 201):** overall open and close, cash, alarm, end-of-night report, and any exception.
- **Bar Supervisor or opening bartender:** bar and floor.
- **Gaming Attendant (when rostered):** gaming room.
- **Head Chef or Kitchen Supervisor (ext. 202):** kitchen open, service, and handover.
- **All staff:** complete your section, tick and initial each item, and tell the Duty Manager if something cannot be done.

## 3. Keys, codes and contacts

- Keys and the alarm fob are issued only to the Duty Manager on shift.
- Safe and alarm codes are never written in this document, shared in chat, or left on a phone.
- Duty Manager: ext. 201
- Kitchen: ext. 202
- Gaming room: ext. 203
- After-hours security contractor: see the Duty Manager contact list on the office noticeboard.
- Emergency: 000

## 4. Typical trading hours

Confirm today's hours with the Duty Manager and the licence posted in the office. Liquor may only be sold during the hours on the licence.

- Monday to Thursday: 10:00 am – 12:00 midnight
- Friday and Saturday: 10:00 am – 12:30 am
- Sunday: 10:00 am – 10:00 pm
- Last drinks are called 15 minutes before close.
- Liquor must not be sold during the 6-hour closure period (see Document 02).

Doors are unlocked at the advertised opening time, not before.

## 5. Opening — Duty Manager

Arrive 30 to 45 minutes before doors.

1. Enter through the staff door and disarm the alarm. If the alarm has been triggered or will not disarm, stay outside, call the after-hours security contractor, and do not enter alone.
2. Turn on house lights, then office lights and computers.
3. Walk the whole venue: bars, dining room, beer garden, amenities, gaming room, kitchen, cool rooms, and stores. Look for damage, leaks, or unsecured doors.
4. Check fire exits are clear and exit lights are on.
5. Confirm the first-aid kit and spill kit are in place.
6. Unlock the office. Open the safe. Prepare the till floats as written on the float sheet.
7. Power on the POS terminals, receipt printers, kitchen printers, and EFTPOS terminals. Confirm each terminal is online.
8. Check the bookings diary, function sheet, and notes from the previous close.
9. Brief the opening team: reservations, 86'd items, maintenance issues, and any banned or caution patrons noted in the incident register.
10. Unlock public entry doors at the advertised opening time.

## 6. Opening — Bar

1. Wash hands. Wear a clean uniform and name badge.
2. Check beer, wine, spirit, and soft-drink levels. Restock from the cool room and spirit store. Tell the Duty Manager if a line or product is out.
3. Run beer lines if required on the line-clean schedule, and check first pours for quality.
4. Cut garnishes. Fill ice wells. Set up glassware, straws, napkins, and cocktail mise en place.
5. Check the glasswasher has salt and rinse aid, and that it is running a clean cycle.
6. Wipe and set bar tops, stools, tables, and the beer garden.
7. Confirm RSA signage is displayed, and that the incident register is in the office.
8. Count the bar float with a second person where possible. Sign the float sheet. Load the till. Do not leave the till unlocked and unattended.
9. Confirm POS and EFTPOS are working with a test or a first sale under the Duty Manager's direction.
10. Tell the Duty Manager the bar is ready to trade.

## 7. Opening — Gaming room

Open the gaming room only if you have a current RCG competency, and only under the Duty Manager.

1. Unlock the gaming room. Turn on lights and air-conditioning.
2. Confirm entry signage, responsible gambling signage, and clocks are in place and visible.
3. Power on machines and the monitoring connection. Wait for machines to come online.
4. Walk each machine: no error lights, screens on, note acceptors clear, stools safe.
5. Check the cash-box area is secure. Do not leave keys in machines.
6. Confirm the gaming float and payout process with the Duty Manager.
7. Confirm the self-exclusion folder is in the office and that listed persons are known to the opening team. Do not discuss a person's exclusion with other patrons.
8. Confirm the gambling incident register is available.
9. Open the gaming room only when the floor is supervised.
10. Tell the Duty Manager the gaming room is open.

If a machine is offline or showing a fault, place an "out of order" sign, record the machine number, and tell the Duty Manager. Do not attempt a technician-level reset.

## 8. Opening — Kitchen

1. The Head Chef or Kitchen Supervisor unlocks the kitchen and cool rooms.
2. Check cool-room and freezer temperatures. Record them on the daily food-safety sheet. If a unit is out of range, tell the Duty Manager and do not use unsafe stock.
3. Read the previous night's handover notes and any discarded or held product.
4. Confirm cleaning was completed. If it was not, do not start prep until the Duty Manager and Head Chef agree a plan.
5. Turn on ovens, grill, fryers, and pass lights in the equipment start-up order. Never leave fryers unattended during heat-up.
6. Set up the pass, printer, and docket spike. Confirm the kitchen printer is receiving a test docket from POS.
7. Prep mise en place to the daily prep list and function sheet.
8. Brief the kitchen team on specials, 86'd items, allergen notes, and bookings.
9. Confirm hand-wash stations, soap, paper towel, and sanitiser are stocked.
10. Tell the Duty Manager the kitchen is ready for service.

## 9. During trade — quick checks

- Tills are never left open or unattended.
- RSA and RCG obligations continue all shift. See Document 02.
- Clean spills immediately and use a wet-floor sign.
- Tell the Duty Manager at once about any refusal of service, injury, fight, suspected minor, self-exclusion breach, or equipment failure that stops trade.

## 10. Closing — Kitchen handover

Start after last food orders, at the time advised by the Duty Manager.

1. Confirm last-orders time for food with the Duty Manager and the pass.
2. Complete outstanding tickets. Do not turn off the kitchen printer until the Duty Manager confirms no tickets are pending.
3. Cool, cover, label, and date leftover product. Discard anything that cannot be safely held. Record discards on the food-safety sheet.
4. Break down the pass and cook line. Clean as you go, then complete the close-down list (grill, fryers, benches, floors, sinks).
5. Turn off cooking equipment in the shutdown order. Leave fryers in a safe state.
6. Check cool-room and freezer temperatures again and record them. Close and latch doors.
7. Take out rubbish and recycling. Replace bin liners. Do not leave the dock door open and unattended.
8. Write the kitchen handover: 86'd items, equipment faults, prep needed for tomorrow, and any incidents (burns, allergens, complaints).
9. Hand the notes to the Duty Manager in person.
10. The last kitchen staff member leaves only after the Duty Manager or Head Chef has checked and locked the kitchen.

## 11. Closing — Bar and floor

1. The Duty Manager announces last drinks in line with the licence and tonight's close time. No liquor is served after licensed hours.
2. Stop cocktail and slow-ticket drinks in time to close cleanly. Complete outstanding tabs.
3. Clear, wash, and put away glassware. Empty and clean ice wells, soda guns, and pour mats. Wipe all surfaces.
4. Restock fridges for the morning where possible. Rotate stock.
5. Run the glasswasher empty cycle. Turn it off. Leave the door ajar to dry.
6. Clean public amenities, the dining room, beer garden, and bar floor.
7. Collect lost property, tag it with the date and where it was found, and leave it in the office.
8. Check the beer garden, car park entry, and amenities for remaining patrons before lock-up.

## 12. Closing — Gaming room

1. Announce closing time clearly. Allow patrons to finish a game in line with venue practice, then politely ask them to leave when the room is closing.
2. Do not pay disputed amounts without the Duty Manager.
3. When the room is empty, walk all machines. Note any errors or damage.
4. Power down, or leave machines in the overnight state set by the Duty Manager, following the posted machine-close procedure.
5. Secure cash-box keys and the gaming float with the Duty Manager.
6. Record any gambling incidents in the gambling incident register.
7. Turn off lights and air-conditioning as posted. Lock the gaming room. Return keys to the Duty Manager.
8. Tell the Duty Manager the gaming room is closed and secured.

## 13. Cash-up and tills

Two people should be present for cash-up where rostered. If you are alone, count in the office with the door closed and CCTV covering the count.

1. Close the floor to new sales. Complete or transfer any open tabs.
2. Run the POS end-of-shift or Z-report for each till. Do not skip a terminal.
3. Remove the till drawer and take it to the office. Never count cash on the bar in view of patrons.
4. Count cash, separate the float, and bag the takings. Complete the cash-up sheet: cash, EFTPOS totals, payouts, comps, and variances.
5. If the variance is more than the amount shown on the cash-up sheet, or you cannot explain it, recount once, then tell the Duty Manager. Do not alter POS reports.
6. Record comps, staff meals, and walkouts as required. Attach dockets.
7. Place takings and the restored float in the safe. The Duty Manager confirms the safe is locked.
8. Lock POS terminals, or power them down as posted. Secure EFTPOS terminals in the office if that is the posted close procedure.
9. Sign and date the cash-up sheet. The Duty Manager countersigns.

## 14. Security lock-up and alarm

The Duty Manager leads lock-up. A second staff member should walk with them when available.

1. Confirm all patrons have left. Check amenities, the beer garden, function rooms, the gaming room, and any hidden corners.
2. Check all windows, fire exits (closed but not blocked), dock doors, bottle-shop door if in use, and the main entry.
3. Turn off non-essential lights, signed-off kitchen equipment, beer-garden heaters, and music. Leave required security and exit lighting on.
4. Leave the CCTV recorder running. Do not unplug it.
5. Lock the office, bottle store, spirit store, gaming room, and kitchen as posted.
6. Complete a final walk. Look for cigarettes, smouldering bins, running taps, and appliances left on.
7. Do a verbal headcount of remaining staff. Do not leave anyone locked inside.
8. Set the alarm from the panel in the office. Leave through the staff door. Confirm the door is locked from outside.
9. If the alarm will not arm, do not ignore the zone. Identify the open door or sensor, fix it if safe, or call the after-hours security contractor. Stay on site until the building is secure or the contractor attends.

## 15. End-of-night report

The Duty Manager completes this report before leaving and files it in the office folder, and on the shared drive if systems are still up.

Include:

1. Date, shift, Duty Manager name, and the trading hours actually used.
2. A short trade summary (quiet / average / busy) and any functions.
3. Cash-up result: each till, EFTPOS, variance, and confirmation the safe is locked.
4. Staff who closed, and any late finish or staff incident.
5. Refusals, evictions, injuries, police or security attendance, and incident-register entry numbers.
6. Equipment faults (POS, EFTPOS, printers, beer lines, cool rooms, gaming machines, AV).
7. Stock issues and 86'd items.
8. Maintenance or cleaning the morning team must do first.
9. Kitchen handover attached or summarised.
10. Anything the opening Duty Manager must know, including banned or caution patrons.

If a serious incident occurred, the Duty Manager also phones the Licensee or Approved Manager before leaving, using the contact list.

Do not skip a safety, cash, or lock-up step to finish faster. If something cannot be completed, note the item, tell the Duty Manager, and record it on this report.

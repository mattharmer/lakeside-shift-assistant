# RSA and Incident Procedures

**The Lakeside Hotel**  
Lake Macquarie, NSW  
Document 02 | Version 1.0 | November 2026  
Internal use only

## 1. Purpose and important notice

This is a workplace procedure for staff at The Lakeside Hotel. It is a general guide only. It is not legal advice.

Staff must follow their current NSW Responsible Service of Alcohol (RSA) training, this venue's liquor licence conditions (posted in the office), and current Liquor & Gaming NSW guidance. If you are unsure, stop and get the Duty Manager (ext. 201). Do not invent a process or guess at the law.

Only staff with a current NSW RSA competency may sell, serve, or supply liquor. Keep it available for inspection while on shift. If it is expired or missing, do not serve liquor and tell the Duty Manager at once.

## 2. What "intoxicated" means

Under the Liquor Act 2007, a person is intoxicated if:

1. their speech, balance, co-ordination, or behaviour is noticeably affected, and
2. it is reasonable in the circumstances to believe this is the result of drinking liquor.

Look at the whole picture. Talk to the person. Consider whether a medical condition, disability, injury, or tiredness could explain what you are seeing. If you are not sure, get the Duty Manager before you refuse or evict.

These signs are a guide. They are not a complete list, and one sign on its own may not mean the person is intoxicated.

- **Speech:** slurred words; rambling, muddled, or incoherent conversation; losing their train of thought; not following a normal conversation.
- **Balance:** unsteady on their feet, swaying, staggering, or stumbling; difficulty walking straight; bumping into furniture or other people; cannot stand, or falling.
- **Co-ordination:** spilling or dropping drinks; fumbling money or having trouble paying; difficulty opening doors; missing their mouth with the glass.
- **Behaviour:** rude, aggressive, argumentative, or offensive; loud, disorderly, or annoying other patrons; overly familiar or making inappropriate advances; drowsy, or sleeping at the bar or a table; vomiting; drinking very rapidly.

Also notice whether the person smells of alcohol, how long they have been drinking, how much you have seen them drink, and whether they arrived already affected. It is better to refuse service on a reasonable belief than to keep serving and hope they are fine. Your reason must not be discriminatory (for example, because of race, sex, or disability).

## 3. Prevent intoxication and refuse service

Do not wait until a person is intoxicated. Offer water and food. Do not run promotions that encourage rapid or excessive drinking. If early signs appear, slow service: no doubles, no shots, and no "one more for the road." Tell the Duty Manager if you are worried about a patron or a group. Do not serve liquor to a person you believe is intoxicated. That is against the law.

If you believe a person is intoxicated, or they cannot produce acceptable ID when asked:

1. Pause. Do not pour the drink.
2. Get a second opinion from the Duty Manager if you can do so quickly and safely. If you cannot, refuse first and then tell the Duty Manager.
3. Speak calmly, privately if possible, and not in front of a crowd. Use the wording in section 4.
4. Do not serve them any more liquor. You may offer water, and food if appropriate.
5. If the person is intoxicated, ask them to leave. Offering to help with a taxi, rideshare, or a sober friend is good practice.
6. Tell nearby bar staff so no one else serves them.
7. If they refuse to leave, follow section 8 and get the Duty Manager immediately.
8. Record the incident in the liquor incident register as soon as practicable, and within 24 hours (see section 7).

Do not use "approaching intoxication" as the reason to evict someone under section 77 of the Liquor Act. Early signs are a reason to slow or refuse further liquor and to get the Duty Manager. Eviction under section 77 is for a person who is intoxicated, violent, quarrelsome, or disorderly, or who falls under the other grounds in that section.

## 4. Refusal wording

Stay calm. Blame the law, not the person. Do not argue, mock, or bargain. Never say you will "drag them out," and do not threaten violence. If they become aggressive, step back, get the Duty Manager, and follow section 8.

**Slowing service**

> "I can get you a water or a soft drink, but I can't do a double. We've got to serve responsibly."

**Refusing further liquor**

> "I'm sorry, I can't serve you any more alcohol tonight. It's the law, and I have to follow it. I can get you a water, and I can help you find a way home."

**Asking the person to leave**

> "I also need to ask you to leave the premises now. You're welcome to wait somewhere safe for a taxi or a friend, but you can't stay on the licensed premises."

**If they argue**

> "I know it's frustrating. I'm not able to change this. If you don't leave when asked, you can be fined. Let me help you get a lift home."

## 5. ID checks

1. Ask for proof of age from anyone who looks under 25, including when they enter, buy liquor, or drink liquor here.
2. Check ID in a well-lit area. Ask the person to take it out of a wallet or case.
3. Take your time. Check the photo against the person, the date of birth (they must be 18 or over), the expiry date, and security features such as holograms. Feel around the photo, date of birth, and edges for signs of tampering.
4. For a digital licence or digital photo card, do not take or handle the person's phone. Ask them to open the official app and show you the document. Follow the current Service NSW (or issuing state) guidance for checking digital ID.
5. If you are not sure it is the same person, or the ID looks wrong, ask for a second acceptable document. If you are still not sure, refuse service and get the Duty Manager.
6. If the person will not, or cannot, produce an acceptable current ID, refuse service and refuse entry where required.

**Acceptable evidence of age** includes a current document with a photo that shows the person is 18 or over:

- a NSW driver licence or NSW digital driver licence (learner, provisional, and full licences)
- a driver licence or digital driver licence issued by another Australian state or territory, or by another country
- an Australian or foreign passport
- a NSW photo card, including a NSW digital photo card
- a proof-of-age card issued by a public authority of the Commonwealth or of another state or territory
- a Keypass identity card issued by Australia Post

Expired documents are not acceptable. Student cards, Medicare cards, credit cards, photocopies, and photos of ID on a phone are not acceptable. Photo cards, driver licences, passports, and Keypass cards can be issued to people under 18 — always check the date of birth.

## 6. Minors

In NSW, a person must be 18 or over to buy or drink liquor on licensed premises. It is also illegal to sell or give liquor to a minor (including secondary supply).

1. Do not serve liquor to a minor, and do not allow an adult to buy liquor for a minor.
2. If you suspect secondary supply, stop, check IDs for the whole group, and get the Duty Manager.
3. Minors are not permitted in the gaming room.
4. Where a minor may be on other parts of the premises (for example, in the dining room with a responsible adult) depends on the licence. Check with the Duty Manager. If in doubt, do not allow the minor to stay, and do not serve liquor to anyone in that group until it is sorted.
5. If a minor is found drinking, trying to buy liquor, or using false ID, refuse service, get the Duty Manager, and record the incident.

## 7. Liquor incident register

This venue keeps a liquor incident register and a separate gambling incident register. The Licensee or Approved Manager is responsible for the liquor register. The Duty Manager maintains it on shift.

A register is required in the situations set out by Liquor & Gaming NSW, including where the premises is authorised to sell liquor after midnight at least once a week on a regular basis, where a licence condition or liquor accord requires it, and where the venue relies on the exemption that allows an intoxicated person to remain only while waiting for medical help or transport home.

Record incidents as soon as practicable, and within 24 hours. Mandatory late-night incidents (between midnight and 5:00 am), and any other incident the licence or current L&GNSW guidance requires, include:

- a person asked to leave under section 77 of the Liquor Act 2007
- violence or anti-social behaviour on the premises, or nearby involving a person who has just left or been refused entry
- a patron needing medical assistance
- an intoxicated person allowed to remain only while waiting for medical assistance or transport home
- suspected possession or use of a prohibited drug or plant

Also record as best practice, at any time of day: refusals of entry or service (intoxication, insufficient or false ID, and minors); injuries and first aid; reports of drink spiking; self-exclusion requests; serious complaints; and compliance issues such as a missing competency card.

Each entry should include the date, time, and location; type of incident; what happened, what action was taken, and the outcome; names or descriptions of people involved and any witnesses; whether police were notified or attended; and the name of the staff member making the entry. Do not use unexplained acronyms.

Mandatory register records must be kept for at least three years. Produce the register if police or a Liquor & Gaming NSW inspector asks for it, and get the Duty Manager immediately. If security is employed on a shift, a separate security register may also be required. Do not combine the two unless the Duty Manager has approved a digital system that meets both requirements. Use the refuse-entry log at the back of the bound register for simple door refusals, and a full incident page for evictions, violence, injury, or anything involving police.

## 8. Intoxicated person who refuses to leave

This follows section 77 of the Liquor Act 2007 at a general level. Get the Duty Manager (ext. 201) as soon as the person will not leave.

**The law, in short**

- An authorised person (the licensee, an employee or agent of the licensee, or a police officer) may refuse to admit, or may turn out, a person who is intoxicated, violent, quarrelsome, or disorderly, or who falls under the other grounds in section 77.
- If that person is on the premises, they must leave when an authorised person requires them to leave (section 77(4)).
- They must not re-enter, or try to re-enter, the premises within 24 hours (section 77(6)).
- They must not remain in, or re-enter, the vicinity of the premises (within 50 metres of the boundary) within 6 hours, unless they have a reasonable excuse (section 77(8)). Reasonable excuses include a reasonable fear for their safety, the need to obtain transport, or that they live in the vicinity.

**What staff must do**

1. Get the Duty Manager. Do not handle this alone if you can avoid it.
2. In a calm, clear voice, require the person to leave. Use the wording in section 4. A second staff member should witness this.
3. Tell them they must leave the premises, must not come back for 24 hours, and must not stay within 50 metres of the venue for 6 hours, unless they need to wait safely for transport, they reasonably fear for their safety, or they live nearby.
4. Offer practical help to leave safely: a taxi or rideshare, a sober friend, or waiting in a safe spot off the premises for a short time if that is appropriate.
5. If they still refuse to leave, or if staff or patrons are not safe, call police. Use 000 if there is violence or immediate danger. Use the NSW Police Assistance Line 131 444 if there is no immediate danger and the Duty Manager agrees that is the right number for the situation.
6. If security is rostered, the Duty Manager may ask them to assist. Staff must not use force. Do not grab, push, or restrain the person. The Act allows reasonable force to turn a person out, but it is this venue's rule that only police (or security acting under the Duty Manager's direction, within their licence) do that work.
7. Stay with other patrons and keep a clear path to the exit. Do not chase the person into the car park or the street if it is unsafe.
8. When the person has left, the Duty Manager records the incident in the register, including the time they were asked to leave, the grounds, what was said, who witnessed it, any offer of transport, and whether police attended. Brief the door and bar so the person is not re-admitted within 24 hours.

If an intoxicated person is waiting for medical help or a ride home, they may be allowed to remain only if the Duty Manager decides the legal conditions for that exemption are met, including the incident-register requirements. Do not make this decision yourself.

## 9. When to call police or security

Call or get the Duty Manager to call **000** immediately if there is violence or a serious threat of violence; a weapon is involved or suspected; a person is unconscious, badly injured, or you believe they need urgent medical help; there is a suspected sexual assault; or you cannot safely contain the situation.

Call police and get the Duty Manager if a person refuses to leave after being required to do so; you suspect prohibited drugs on the premises; a minor is involved in a liquor offence and the Duty Manager directs you to report it; there is a report of drink spiking; or staff feel unsafe.

If security is rostered, the Duty Manager decides when they are called to the floor. Do not leave your post unattended to find security without telling another staff member. Preserve the scene as much as is safe if police have been called to a violent incident. Do not clean away evidence unless you must do so for safety.

After any incident: make sure staff and patrons are safe; tell the Duty Manager everything you saw and did while it is fresh; complete the incident register within 24 hours; do not discuss the incident on social media or with other patrons; and if you are shaken or hurt, tell the Duty Manager before you go back on the floor.

## 10. Trading hours, the 6-hour closure, and lockouts

1. Sell liquor only during the hours on this venue's licence. The operational close times are in Document 01, but the licence always comes first.
2. A 6-hour closure period applies to liquor licences in the circumstances set out in the Liquor Act 2007. Unless this venue's licence states a different period, that closure is typically 4:00 am to 10:00 am. During the 6-hour closure, liquor must not be sold by retail on the premises or for takeaway. If a staying guest asks for liquor during that period, get the Duty Manager — do not serve it yourself.
3. There is no statewide lockout (last-entry curfew) that automatically applies to a hotel in Lake Macquarie. Former Sydney precinct lockout conditions do not apply here. Follow any lockout, last-entry, or other special condition written on this venue's licence. If you are not sure, ask the Duty Manager and do not guess.

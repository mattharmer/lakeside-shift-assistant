# POS, Network and AV Troubleshooting

**The Lakeside Hotel**  
Lake Macquarie, NSW  
Document 04 | Version 1.0 | November 2026  
Internal use only

## 1. Purpose

Use these steps when payments, POS, printers, the network, TVs, or the music system fail during trade. The aim is to get service moving again safely, or to escalate quickly.

## 2. Rules before you start

1. Do not guess at passwords, safe codes, or router logins. Those details are held by the Duty Manager only and are not written in this document.
2. Do not take card numbers down on paper, in a phone, or over the phone. If card payments are down, the Duty Manager decides whether to use a backup terminal or move to cash only.
3. Do not unplug the CCTV recorder or the main network gear unless the Duty Manager is doing a planned restart.
4. Tell the Duty Manager (ext. 201) if a fix will take more than a few minutes, if payments are down, or if kitchen tickets have stopped.
5. When you escalate, say what you can see, what you have already tried, and which terminal, printer, or screen is affected.

**Contacts**

- Duty Manager: ext. 201
- Kitchen: ext. 202
- IT support: the Duty Manager calls from the office contact list. Staff do not ring IT directly unless the Duty Manager asks them to.

## 3. EFTPOS terminal will not connect

**What you might see:** "No connection", "offline", a spinning wait screen, or a terminal that will not start a sale.

1. Check the terminal is powered on and the screen is lit. If it is on a base or cable, confirm the cable is seated at both ends.
2. Check there is paper in the terminal and the cover is closed. A paper error can look like a failed sale.
3. Look for a leftover prompt from the last transaction. Cancel or complete it only if the screen is clearly asking you to, then try again.
4. If the terminal uses Wi-Fi or a mobile connection, confirm other nearby terminals or staff phones have a signal. If nothing has signal, go to section 7.
5. Ask the guest to wait. Do not keep retrying a card in a way that could double-charge. Check the last receipt or the POS sale to see if the payment already went through.
6. Restart the EFTPOS terminal only: turn it off (or hold the power key as marked on the device), wait 30 seconds, turn it back on, and wait until it is idle before trying one test transaction.
7. If it still fails, try a different EFTPOS terminal, or move the sale to another bar or the office terminal if one is online.
8. If no EFTPOS terminal will connect, tell the Duty Manager immediately. Payments may need to go cash-only until the link is back.
9. Do not reboot the whole network yourself. The Duty Manager follows section 7 if a network restart is needed.
10. If the terminal is physically damaged, or a card is stuck, stop using it, put an "out of order" note on it, and get the Duty Manager.

**Escalate now if:** payments are down on all terminals, a guest may have been charged twice, or a card is trapped.

## 4. POS terminal frozen or offline

**What you might see:** the screen does not respond, a sale is stuck, or the terminal is offline and will not log on.

1. Stop tapping the screen. Give it 20 to 30 seconds in case it is catching up.
2. If a payment is mid-way, do not pull the power. Watch the EFTPOS terminal and any printed docket. Note the table or ticket number and the last amount shown.
3. Check the network cables at the back of the terminal and at the wall or switch if you can reach them safely. Do not unplug other terminals to "test".
4. If the terminal is frozen and no payment is in progress, restart that POS terminal only: sign out if you can, then power it off, wait 30 seconds, power it on, and log back in as usual.
5. After it restarts, confirm you can open a table or a sale, send a test docket if needed, and take a payment.
6. If the terminal stays offline, move service to another POS if one is working and tell the Duty Manager.
7. Write down any sales that may not have saved. Do not re-key a sale unless the Duty Manager has confirmed it did not go through.
8. If several POS terminals are offline at once, treat it as a network issue (section 7) and get the Duty Manager.

**Escalate now if:** more than one POS is down, a sale may be missing or duplicated, or the kitchen has stopped receiving tickets.

## 5. Receipt or docket printer not printing

**What you might see:** no receipt, a blank page, a jam, or the POS saying the printer is offline.

1. Check the printer has power and that the power point is on.
2. Open the lid. Check there is paper, that it is feeding from the right direction, and that the lid is closed firmly.
3. If paper is jammed, turn the printer off, clear the jam gently, reload paper, close the lid, and turn it back on. Do not use a knife in the printer.
4. Check the cable between the printer and the POS, or the network cable if it is a network printer.
5. From the POS, confirm the receipt printer is the selected printer for that terminal. If you are not sure how, get the Duty Manager rather than changing settings at random.
6. Restart the printer, wait until it is ready, then reprint the last receipt or docket from the POS.
7. If it still will not print, write the guest's order or the amount on a notepad for your records, give the guest a handwritten receipt if they need one, and get the Duty Manager.
8. Do not keep sending the same print job in a loop. It may all come out at once when the printer recovers.

## 6. Kitchen printer not getting orders

**What you might see:** the floor POS accepts the order, but nothing prints in the kitchen, or only some items print.

1. Phone the kitchen (ext. 202) and tell them tickets may be missing. Do not assume the dish is being cooked.
2. Check the kitchen printer has power, paper, and a closed lid, and that no one has switched it off during a clean.
3. Clear any jam. Confirm the printer is online (lights as normal).
4. From a POS, send a test docket or a known test item if the Duty Manager agrees, and stand at the printer to see if it arrives.
5. Check other floor terminals. If only one POS cannot send to the kitchen, restart that POS (section 4) and try again.
6. If no POS can reach the kitchen printer, get the Duty Manager and treat it as a printer or network fault.
7. Until it is fixed, take food orders on a duplicate docket or notepad, walk them to the pass, and tell the Head Chef. Number the tickets so nothing is lost.
8. When the printer is back, do not resend a whole batch of old tickets unless the kitchen asks. Confirm with the pass first so meals are not doubled.

**Escalate now if:** food service is delayed, a function is in progress, or tickets have been lost.

## 7. Wi-Fi or network down

**What you might see:** POS or EFTPOS offline, no staff Wi-Fi, or payments and kitchen tickets failing at the same time.

1. Confirm what is down: EFTPOS only, POS only, kitchen printers, staff Wi-Fi, or everything. This helps the Duty Manager decide the next step.
2. Check the network cabinet or shelf (in the office) for lights on the NBN/modem, the router, and the switch. Do not unplug anything yet.
   - If there are no lights at all, check the power point and any power-board switch.
   - If the modem lights are in a fault state (for example, no "online" or broadband light), it may be a provider outage.
3. See whether a nearby staff device has internet. If nothing does, it is likely a venue or provider issue, not one terminal.
4. Tell floor and kitchen that payments or tickets may be delayed.
5. Only the Duty Manager restarts network gear. Staff must not do this on their own.
6. If a restart is needed, the Duty Manager uses this power-cycle order:
   1. Tell staff that the network will drop for a few minutes.
   2. Power off the switch, then the router, then the NBN/modem. Leave POS and EFTPOS as they are unless the Duty Manager also restarts them later.
   3. Wait 60 seconds.
   4. Power on the NBN/modem first. Wait until its lights are stable (about two minutes).
   5. Power on the router. Wait about two minutes.
   6. Power on the switch. Wait one minute.
   7. Restart any POS or EFTPOS terminal that is still offline, one at a time.
7. When the network is back, test: one POS login, one EFTPOS sale or test, and one kitchen test docket.
8. If the network will not recover, the Duty Manager calls IT support from the contact list and, if needed, the internet provider. Until then, use the backup plan: cash-only if cards are down, handwritten or walked tickets to the kitchen, and a note of every sale for later entry.

Do not factory-reset a router or switch. Do not share the Wi-Fi admin login. Guest Wi-Fi, if it is offered, is separate from the POS network — turning guest Wi-Fi off or on will not usually fix payments.

## 8. TV or AV — no signal

**What you might see:** a black or blue screen, "no signal", the wrong input, or sound with no picture (or picture with no sound).

1. Confirm the TV or screen actually has power. Check the wall point and whether the screen is on standby.
2. Check the input or source (HDMI 1, HDMI 2, AV, and so on). Cycle to the input used for that screen. The usual input for each screen is on the small label on the bezel or remote caddy, if labelled.
3. Confirm the set-top box, sports box, or playback device is on, and that its own screen or lights look normal. Restart that device if it is frozen, then wait for it to boot.
4. Check the HDMI or AV cable at both ends if you can reach it safely. Do not climb on furniture. Get the Duty Manager if a ladder or the AV cupboard is needed.
5. If there is picture but no sound, check the TV mute, the TV volume, and whether sound is routed through the music or amplifier system instead of the TV speakers.
6. If several screens are down at once, check the main AV rack or splitter in the AV cupboard with the Duty Manager. Do not pull a handful of leads to "see what happens".
7. For a scheduled race, match, or function, tell the Duty Manager early so a backup screen or source can be tried.

If a screen is used for gaming-room or statutory signage, tell the Duty Manager straight away. Do not leave required signage off.

## 9. Music system

**What you might see:** no music, music in some rooms only, volume stuck, or the wrong source.

1. Check whether the system is muted, or the zone volume for that room is turned down (bar, dining, beer garden, amenities).
2. Confirm the source is correct (the usual streaming unit, PC, or player in the office or AV cupboard). If the player is paused, frozen, or has lost the network, restart the player only.
3. If one zone is dead and others are fine, check the zone local volume control and whether a speaker isolator or patio switch has been turned off.
4. Do not turn the music up past the posted house level, especially in the beer garden. If a neighbour complaint comes in, get the Duty Manager and turn that zone down.
5. If you cannot restore sound in a few minutes, get the Duty Manager. Leave the system in a safe, quiet state rather than leaving a loud blast after a restart.

## 10. When to escalate

Get the **Duty Manager (ext. 201)** immediately if:

- all EFTPOS or all POS terminals are down
- kitchen tickets have stopped
- you think a guest has been charged twice, or a sale may be missing
- a network restart is needed
- you would need a password, a ladder, or to open the network cabinet or AV rack
- the fault lasts more than a few minutes during busy trade or a function

The Duty Manager escalates to **IT support** (office contact list) if the steps above have failed, or if the fault involves the server, router login, a provider outage, or a hardware replacement. Until help arrives, keep service moving with the backup plan in section 7 and write down what happened for the end-of-night report.

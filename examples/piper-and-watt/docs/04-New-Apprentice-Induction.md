# New Apprentice Induction

**Piper & Watt Plumbing & Electrical**  
Newcastle and Lake Macquarie, NSW  
Document 04 | Version 1.0 | November 2026  
Internal use only

## 1. Welcome

Welcome to Piper & Watt Plumbing & Electrical. We look after plumbing, drainage, and electrical work for homes and small businesses across Newcastle and Lake Macquarie.

This pack covers your first day and first weeks. Read it before you start. Bring any questions to your licensed supervisor — plumbing on ext. 2, electrical on ext. 3 — or to the office on ext. 1. There are no silly questions on day one.

## 2. Before your first day

Complete these with the office or the person named in your start email:

1. Paperwork: tax file number declaration, superannuation details, bank account, emergency contact, and right-to-work documents.
2. Your training contract and, if you are on construction sites, a current white card (general construction induction).
3. A current driver's licence if you will drive a company vehicle. You may only drive once the office has authorised you.
4. PPE sizes: steel-cap boots, polo, trousers, glasses, gloves, and any other item the office lists for your trade.
5. If you are unwell, do not come in. Phone the office and your supervisor as soon as you know. Do not send a last-minute text and assume it has been seen.

Bring your apprentice identification, white card, and any other tickets with you every day.

## 3. Your first day

1. Arrive at the yard 15 minutes before your start time. Park in the staff parks, not in the driveway the vans use to leave.
2. Sign on as the office shows you, then introduce yourself to the office, your licensed supervisor, and the Workshop Leading Hand (ext. 4).
3. You will be buddied with a licensed tradesperson. Stay with them unless the supervisor moves you.
4. Walk the yard with your buddy: amenities, first-aid kit, spill kit, SDS folder, lock-out station, loading area, and the assembly point, which is at the front gate.
5. Leave bags and valuables in the staff room. The company is not responsible for items left unsecured.
6. Phones stay off the job except for the job app, maps, or an emergency, or if your supervisor asks you to make a call.
7. At the end of the day, complete your timesheet, help restock, return keys, put the tablet on charge, and tell your supervisor how the day went and anything you are unsure about.

## 4. Uniform and PPE

Wear the issued PPE on every job, and replace anything damaged before you leave the yard.

- Company polo or work shirt, clean and in good repair.
- Work trousers, not shorts, unless the supervisor has approved them for a specific task.
- Steel-cap boots, laced and in good condition.
- Safety glasses, work gloves, and hearing protection, as required for the task.
- A sun hat and sunscreen for outdoor work. Use them.
- High-visibility clothing as issued, and as required on construction sites.
- A harness and lanyard only if they have been issued to you and you have been trained and signed off. Do not borrow one and improvise.

Keep hair, jewellery, and loose clothing from being caught in tools or plant. If an item of PPE does not fit or is damaged, tell the office before you go out.

## 5. Vehicle and van stock

- You may only drive a company vehicle if you hold the right licence and the office has authorised you. Follow road rules, including phone and speed rules. No unauthorised passengers.
- Before you leave the yard, do a daily check with your buddy or driver: tyres, lights, oil, coolant, windscreen, registration label, first-aid kit, fire extinguisher, spill kit, and that the van is tidy and lockable.
- Fuel only on the company fuel card, as the office has shown you. Keep the receipt in the van folder.
- If you are in a crash, or the van is damaged or has a flat tyre, make the scene safe, call 000 if anyone is hurt, then phone your licensed supervisor and the office. Do not argue with the other driver.
- Keep the van locked when you are not at it. Do not leave tools, keys, or the tablet on the back step.

A typical van carries, as a minimum:

- The tools and testers for that trade. Electrical testers are only used if you have been signed off, and only under a licensed electrician.
- Common fittings and consumables for the day's jobs, plus the parts listed on the job cards.
- PPE for the crew on that van, isolation locks and tags, and the spill kit.
- The job-app tablet, charged, and the SDS folder.
- A first-aid kit, fire extinguisher, cones or barriers as issued, and a sharps container.

If something is missing, tell the Workshop Leading Hand (ext. 4) before you leave. At the end of the day, restock what you used and note anything that needs ordering.

## 6. Timesheets and the job app

1. Complete your timesheet in the job app every day: start, finish, breaks, travel, and the job numbers you worked on.
2. Your supervisor approves the timesheet. If you forget a day, tell them the same week. Do not guess times.
3. There are no cash jobs, after-hours favours, or work off the books. All work goes through the app.
4. Job-card workflow, in order:
   1. Accept the job and read the notes.
   2. Travel, then mark arrived on site.
   3. Complete the pre-start (Document 02) before you begin.
   4. Take photos before and after, as the job requires.
   5. Record parts used, time, and any variation the supervisor has approved.
   6. Get the customer signature and any payment direction the office has set.
   7. Mark departed, write a short handover note, and return unused parts to the van.

Stay inside the agreed scope. If the customer wants extra work, call the supervisor before you start it.

## 7. What you may and may not do

- You work under supervision. You do not certify electrical work, sign compliance documents, or give a customer a firm quote that has not been approved (see Document 01).
- You do not isolate electrical supply, test for dead, work at height, or enter a confined space on your own (see Document 02).
- You do not give customers do-it-yourself electrical advice, or talk them through wiring, adding a power point, or opening a switchboard. If they ask, offer to book a licensed electrician, and use only the emergency wording in Document 03 if there is danger.
- If you suspect asbestos, stop and call your supervisor. Do not cut or drill to "get it done."
- If you are unsure, stop and ask. That is always acceptable.

## 8. Conduct and fit for work

- Be on time. If you will be late or cannot work, phone the office and your supervisor as soon as you know.
- You must be fit for work. Illegal drugs have no place on site. If you are impaired, you will be stood down and the supervisor will take it further.
- Treat customers and colleagues courteously. Bullying, harassment, and discrimination are not accepted. Tell your supervisor if you feel unsafe or if you see this happen.
- Do not post photos of customer homes, vans, incidents, or colleagues on social media. Do not use a personal phone to photograph a customer's property.
- Smoking and vaping only in a designated area, off the customer's frontage, and on a break.
- Know your nearest exit on every site. In a fire, medical emergency, or violence, call 000 if needed, then get your supervisor.

The full policies are in the office. You will be asked to sign that you have read the key ones in your first week.

## 9. Who to call

Start with the person closest to the task, then your licensed supervisor if you are still unsure.

- **Office / Scheduler (ext. 1):** roster, timesheets, bookings, payment, cancellations, and the van if you cannot reach the workshop.
- **Licensed Plumbing Supervisor (ext. 2):** plumbing and drainage work, hot water, gas, and plumbing safety questions.
- **Licensed Electrical Supervisor (ext. 3):** electrical work, isolation, switchboards, smoke alarms, and electrical safety questions.
- **Workshop Leading Hand (ext. 4):** van stock, tools, yard, and vehicle checks.
- **Your buddy or the licensed tradesperson on the job:** how this site runs, and "is this normal?" questions.
- **Emergency:** 000, then your supervisor.

After hours, use the on-call supervisor on the roster on the office board. Do not publish a personal mobile number in a message or on a job card left with a customer.

If you cannot get through on an extension, call the office and ask them to patch you. Do not leave a safety question sitting in a text.

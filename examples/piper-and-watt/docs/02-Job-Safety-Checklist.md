# Job Safety Checklist

**Piper & Watt Plumbing & Electrical**  
Newcastle and Lake Macquarie, NSW  
Document 02 | Version 1.0 | November 2026  
Internal use only

## 1. Purpose

Complete this pre-start on every job, before work begins. Use it with the job card in the app.

This is a workplace procedure for Piper & Watt staff. It is not a substitute for a job-specific Safe Work Method Statement (SWMS) on high-risk work, and it is not legal advice.

## 2. Who is responsible

- **Every tradesperson and apprentice on site:** complete the pre-start, wear the required PPE, stop if the job is unsafe or outside your ticket, and call the supervisor rather than pushing on.
- **Licensed Plumbing Supervisor (ext. 2) and Licensed Electrical Supervisor (ext. 3):** decide whether a job-specific SWMS, a permit, or extra controls are needed, and handle regulator notifications.
- **Office / Scheduler (ext. 1):** pass on known site risks when booking the job, and do not send an apprentice to a high-risk job unsupervised.
- **Apprentices:** work only under the supervision required for the task. Do not isolate electrical supply, test for dead, enter a confined space, or work at height on your own.

## 3. NSW work health and safety — general

We have a duty to provide a safe workplace and to carry out work safely under NSW work health and safety law, including the Work Health and Safety Act 2011 (NSW) and the Work Health and Safety Regulation 2017 (NSW). SafeWork NSW is the regulator.

This document does not quote section numbers or invent licence conditions. If you need the legal detail, ask the licensed supervisor and refer to current SafeWork NSW guidance.

High-risk construction work — including work at heights where a person can fall, work on or near live electrical installations, work in a confined space, and work that may disturb asbestos — needs a job-specific SWMS, competent people, and supervisor sign-off. If the right SWMS is not in place, do not start. Call the relevant licensed supervisor.

## 4. Pre-start — every job

Complete these before you begin, and record them on the pre-start in the job app.

1. You are fit for work. PPE is on, and your licences and tickets for this task are current and with you.
2. You understand the job card and the agreed scope. If the customer asks for extra work, stop and get the supervisor to approve a variation.
3. On a construction site, complete the site induction and follow the principal contractor's rules.
4. Walk the site: access and egress, children and pets, traffic, overhead power, wet or uneven ground, fragile roofs, and other trades.
5. Identify the main hazards and the controls you will use. Write them on the pre-start.
6. If electrical work or plant isolation is involved, plan the isolation and lock-out/tag-out before you touch the installation (see section 6).
7. Know the emergency plan: call 000 in a dangerous emergency, know how you will get off the site, and confirm the first-aid kit and fire extinguisher are in the van.
8. If anything is outside your ticket, the scope is unclear, or you are unsure, stop and call the relevant licensed supervisor. Do not start.

## 5. Working at heights

- Prefer work from the ground, or from an elevated work platform, over a ladder where that is practicable.
- Ladders must be industrial rated, inspected before use, set up on a firm, level base, and used with three points of contact. Do not over-reach. Do not stand on the top cap or the last safe rung.
- Before you get on a roof, check the pitch, the condition of the sheets, skylights and brittle areas, weather, and the edge protection or harness system the supervisor has directed for that job.
- Do not work on a roof in high wind, rain, or if the roof looks fragile, until the licensed supervisor has cleared it.
- Keep clear of overhead power lines. If a line is close to the work, stop and call the supervisor. Do not guess the distance or the safe method.
- Apprentices do not work at height unsupervised.

## 6. Live electrical isolation and lock-out/tag-out

- Treat every circuit as live until a licensed electrician has proved it dead.
- Apprentices do not isolate supply, test for dead, or work on or near live parts except under the direct supervision of a licensed electrician, and never on their own.
- Isolation, in outline, is: identify the circuit, isolate it at the board, apply your personal lock and tag, then prove the tester, test for dead, and prove the tester again.
- Do not rely on a light switch, a circuit label alone, or a handwritten note on the board.
- If you cannot lock the isolator, stop and call the Licensed Electrical Supervisor (ext. 3).
- Restoration is only by the licensed electrician, once the work is complete and safe.
- Never give a customer instructions to open a switchboard, isolate a circuit, test for dead, or add wiring. If there is immediate danger, use only the emergency wording in Document 03, then tell them to wait for us or call 000.

## 7. Asbestos awareness

- Buildings built or renovated up to the mid-1980s, and some later work, may contain asbestos. Common places include eaves, backing boards, vinyl floor, pipes, lagging, and older switchboard panels.
- Do not cut, drill, scrape, sand, or break material if you suspect it may contain asbestos.
- Stop, keep other people away, and call the licensed supervisor. Do not improvise a "careful" cut.
- Removal is carried out by a licensed asbestos removalist. Piper & Watt does not remove asbestos unless the business has arranged a licensed contractor.
- If a switchboard may have an asbestos backing, the licensed electrician decides the method. Apprentices do not disturb it.

## 8. Confined spaces

- Pits, tanks, some manholes, vessels, and some roof cavities may be confined spaces.
- Do not enter a confined space unless you are trained, a job-specific SWMS and entry permit are in place, and the licensed supervisor has authorised that entry.
- Never enter a sewer, tank, or pit to "quickly have a look."

## 9. Other common hazards

- **Drains and biological material:** wear the gloves and eye protection issued for this work. Wash up before you eat or touch your face. Sharps go in the sharps container in the van; tell the supervisor.
- **Gas:** if you smell gas, do not switch electrical items on or off, do not use a naked flame, ventilate if it is safe, get people away, and call the Licensed Plumbing Supervisor. If there is immediate danger, call 000.
- **Traffic and public areas:** set out cones or barriers as directed, and keep tools and hoses from becoming a trip hazard.
- **Dust, chemicals and hot work:** use the controls and PPE the supervisor has set for that task. Read the safety data sheet in the van folder before you use an unfamiliar product.
- **Animals and occupants:** ask for dogs to be secured. Watch for children around open pits, ladders, and the van.
- **Lone work:** on a lone job, follow the check-in process in the job app. If you cannot check in, the office will call you.

## 10. If something goes wrong

1. If anyone is in danger or seriously hurt, call **000** first. Make the area safe only if you can do so without putting yourself at risk.
2. Give first aid if you are able, and stay with the person until help arrives.
3. Contact the relevant licensed supervisor immediately. The office (ext. 1) can help make the calls if you cannot get through.
4. Do not disturb the scene of a serious incident if the supervisor or SafeWork NSW may need to inspect it, other than to save life or prevent further harm. The licensed supervisor handles any notification to the regulator.
5. Record what happened on the job card as soon as you can, and do not discuss the incident on social media or with journalists.

If you are unsure at any point, stop the job and call the supervisor. That is always the right call.
